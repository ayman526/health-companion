# Copy guide: how Health Journal / سجلك الصحي talks

The two languages are written separately, not translated. Each should sound
like it was written first in that language, for people in that country.

## Both languages

- Short. One idea per sentence. Cut words that don't change the meaning.
- Talk to the person ("your results", "نتائجك"). Warm, calm, never alarming.
- Buttons are verbs: "Add medication" / "أضف دواء".
- Health safety wording keeps its meaning exactly: never shorten away "talk to
  your doctor", emergency guidance, or what the app does not do.
- Numbers in Western digits (0-9) in both languages.

## English (USA)

- Plain American English, sentence case, contractions ("you'll", "can't").
- No "please", "successfully", "kindly". No exclamation marks.
- "Medications" (not "medicines") in labels; "doctor", "lab results",
  "prescription", "blood sugar", "check-in".
- Errors say what happened and what to do: "Couldn't save. Try again."

## Arabic (Egypt)

- Simple, modern Arabic (فصحى مبسّطة) that reads naturally in Egypt. No heavy
  dialect, but no stiff formal phrasing either.
- Avoid: "يُرجى"، "قم بـ"، "تمّ بنجاح"، "جارٍ" when a shorter word works,
  long chains of إضافة.
- Words people in Egypt use: روشتة، تحاليل، الدكتور/الطبيب، علبة الدواء،
  السكر، الضغط، الكشف، المعمل.
- Greetings and small talk can lean Egyptian-friendly: "أهلًا يا {{name}}".
- Address the person directly; the masculine form is the default when the
  sentence needs a gender.
